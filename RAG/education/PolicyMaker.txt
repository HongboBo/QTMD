National policy requires reducing the rural–urban education gap within 10 years.

Education budgets are limited to ~4% of GDP.

Each rural school upgrade costs $1–2 million on average.

International studies (OECD, World Bank) link equitable funding to higher social mobility.

Urban schools drive innovation and international test scores.

Investing in rural schools reduces dropout rates and rural–urban migration.

Cutting urban funding risks angering urban taxpayers and parents.

40% of government education projects already target rural schools.

Urban areas generate over 70% of tax revenue but receive less than half of funding per capita.

Teacher salary incentives strongly affect whether they choose rural or urban posts.

ICT investment is considered the fastest way to narrow the gap.

Regional quotas in college entrance exams aim to benefit rural students.

Policy must balance fairness (equity) and efficiency (maximizing outcomes).

Education is seen as a long-term driver of GDP growth.

Misallocation of funds can spark political and public backlash.
