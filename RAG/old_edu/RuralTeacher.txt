Teacher turnover is high in rural schools, with many young teachers leaving after 1–2 years.

Rural students often come from low-income families and cannot afford extracurricular tutoring.

Teaching facilities in rural schools are outdated, with limited computers and lab equipment.

Urban students have greater access to extracurricular resources.

National policy on “balanced compulsory education” emphasizes increased investment in rural education.

Some rural schools have been merged, forcing students to travel longer distances.

Training opportunities are more abundant in cities; rural teachers lack professional development support.

Many rural schools operate boarding systems, but dormitory conditions are poor.

College admission rates in rural areas are lower than in urban areas.

Limited internet and digital equipment hinder online learning.

Rural schools often face the problem of left-behind children lacking parental care.

When public funding is insufficient, rural schools are disproportionately affected.

Urban schools have safer buildings and better sports facilities than rural schools.

Mental health issues among rural students often go unnoticed.

Rural schools lack resources for arts and music education.
